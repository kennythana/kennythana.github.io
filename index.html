In progress

Wearable ECG + PPG band for pulse arrival time

My grandmother’s stroke is why I care about monitoring. After a stroke, heart rhythm and blood pressure get checked constantly, and both leave signals at the skin. This band records an electrical signal (ECG) and an optical one (PPG) at the same time. The delay between them, the pulse arrival time, tracks how fast the pressure wave travels, which is one route to cuff-less blood-pressure estimates.

The goal is a validated measurement, not a gadget. Heart rate from the band is compared against a commercial watch using Bland–Altman agreement analysis.

  • HardwareArduino Uno · AD8232 single-lead ECG · MAX30102 red/IR PPG, ~200 Hz
  • SoftwareFirmware and a Python live-plot logger written
  • AnalysisBand-pass filtering, R-peak and pulse-foot detection, PAT, HRV (RMSSD)
  • NextBreadboard build, rest and post-exercise sessions, agreement vs. watch

Biomaterials · Chiang Mai University

Mycelium-based green composites

Fungal mycelium grown through agricultural and industrial waste, then pressed into usable materials. As first author, I showed that paper waste improves the composites’ physical and mechanical properties. Later papers apply the same materials to sawdust from wood processing, interior panels and egg packaging.

Nanomaterials · BU Yang Lab

Gold nanorods tuned to 1030 nm

Synthesizing gold nanorods whose absorption peak sits at 1030 nm, in the near-infrared. They’re the base material for photoacoustic retinal neuromodulation, which the lab is developing toward an eyedrop for retinitis pigmentosa and age-related macular degeneration.

About

Bangkok · Singapore · Boston

I’m a biomedical engineering student at Boston University. I grew up in Bangkok and Singapore, and I’ve spent most of the last eight years studying abroad.

My research started with materials: turning agricultural waste into composites people can use, and then making nanoparticles that respond to near-infrared light. Wearable biosensing is where those two threads meet. A sensor on the skin is a materials problem and an optics problem at once.

Outside the lab I shoot film and digital photography. I scan and edit my own negatives, and I’ve photographed for BU. Working with a camera taught me most of what I know about light before any optics class did.
